Trip Details

Nestled in the Taos ski valley village is the popular trail and hike to Wheeler Peak, the highest point in New Mexico at 13,159 ft. There was a small chance of thunderstorms in the afternoon so I got an early start at about 6 am, planning to reach the peak then continue clockwise around the mountain ridges and end up back at the trailhead. Trying to be careful I opted out of doing the circle because of questionable blue and grey clouds that formed pretty fast. The hike is well graded with many switchbacks which helps counterbalance the higher elevation. Along the way were many pika, a marmot or two, and a large herd of bighorn sheep near the next peak over, Mt Walter. After taking in the views on Wheeler Peak I continued south to Simpson Peak where there was a sign noting travel further south is not permitted since it is part of the Taos Pueblo wilderness, so I turned around and trekked back to the trailhead, gazing out across the folds of mountains, fast moving clouds, and fine views. It was a fun hike, short enough for most people but somewhat of a challenge due to the elevation and uphill gain. It would be fun to return to the area sometime. North of NM-150 which is the road up to the Taos ski valley is the Columbine-Hondo wilderness which seems to have quite a few trails with probably much fewer crowds and some backpacking trip potential.

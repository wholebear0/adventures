Trip Details

Starting from the top of Mt Lemmon, this hike followed along the Meadow trail, then the Sutherland trail, then Canada del Oro trail for a bit, then finally the Samaniego Ridge trail to Samaniego Peak. These trails dont get a lot of use and seem to have a reputation as being overgrown but I think they are among the best among the high elevation hikes in the Mt Lemmon vicinity. Not only because of the views but because of the peace and solitude with mostly zero crowds. This hike was no different as I saw not a single other person.

The route itself was in great condition and easy to follow except for one or two brief sections of 50 feet of trail near drainages that were a bushwhack. But they were no more inconvenient than just pushing some plants aside. I kept hiking along through tall grass on both sides, then underneath pine trees on a carpet of pine needles as clouds began rolling in, then along the Samaniego ridge which offered great views of Oro Valley to the west and the Canada del Oro canyon to the east. On the way to Samaniego peak I passed some bear poo and on the way back there were at least a dozen bear prints on the trail in the same area. But just east of Samaniego peak at the somewhat murky Walnut spring was a sign stuck in the ground saying end of trail. At this point I jumped off trail and headed west, initially finding some easy walking through tall grass and weaving around bushes. But as the peak got closer and slope steeper, the pace slowed substantially. The bushes were larger and more stubborn and there were numerous boulders, all at a kind of steep gradient. Inevitably I got scratched up but made it to the top ridgeline just north of the peak. There were some big boulders between the peak and myself so I went down a few feet off the ridge and found it much easier, making it over to the peak in a few minutes. After the short scrambling there were great 360 views.

Of interest was the area directly east, the Canada del Oro canyon and towards Oracle, an even more remote area I dont know much about and which probably receives even fewer visitors. There is a trail down along the canyon bottom but following along water and with shade I imagine it could be quite overgrown with dense vegetation, but there is no way of knowing for sure until checking it out some day.

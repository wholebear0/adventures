Trip Details

This was a fun one. The Sinister is a seldom visited remote peak, possibly because of remoteness, lack of any trail, and it being a somewhat technical peak. The mountain is covered with cool pinnacles and interesting rock. I followed a route I found online by Bob Byrd (https://www.snwburd.com/bob/trip_reports/sinister_1.html) who approached via the west side. He did a short but steep route that started from the road south of Lower Sunset lake. It's a good place to start because there is a deep rut in the road south of Lower Sunset lake just after taking a right turn off sunset lake road. A basic 2wd SUV or car won't make it across. High clearance and probably 4wd is necessary.

The road walk is short and I eventually headed off, venturing through forest which gave way to dirt slopes near the base of the mountains. From there on the rock is loose and somewhat poor quality, always loose and sliding out beneath each footstep, increasing the difficulty of the hike. I hiked up a rib gaining elevation fast and was pleased with the hike and progress made so far without much bushwhacking. After the rib the slope steepened significantly and the footing became a combination of loose dirt, gravel, and small rocks alongside numerous cool rock pinnacles. Ahead and upwards the Sinister loomed in the distance, growing closer by the step. I aimed for the notch between the two large pinnacles at the top. Like the morning it was extremely windy and I worried about safety of the crux of the route, an exposed scramble up to the peak.

At the notch the views out over to the other side and back already made the hike worth it. Fortunately, the big pinnacles of the peak blocked much of the wind so I continued scrambling up following Bob's route, past a 20-30ft crux of exposed class 3+ to 4. Following was a 7 foot ledge requiring a chimney move, then immediately past it a chokestone. I slipped under the stone as it was the only way past but there was a steep slope with loose dirt so it took a minute to climb up and out of it. My pant pockets filled with dirt and sand. With the most difficult section done, the last part was some more fun maybe 100ft scrambling and light stemming up a funnel of a chute where upon exiting, I was at the summit. The views out in all directions were fantastic and there wasn't a single person in sight. The surrounding Mokelumn wilderness was very scenic with many interesting geographic features. I would like to come back to hike more of it. The hike down took only an hour as I basically slid down the steep gravel and sand quickly as if they were sand dunes. This was a fantastic and fun off trail hike and one to remember.

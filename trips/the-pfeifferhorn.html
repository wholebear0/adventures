Trip Details

The Pfeifferhorn is a 10 mile hike with about 4,000 ft of vertical gain in the Lone Peak wilderness next to Salt Lake City. Getting to the trailhead involves a nice drive up through cottonwood canyon. At 7 am on a Thursday the trailhead was already almost entirely full. The first half of the hike goes by quick, gaining moderate elevation through trees. The noise of cars fades away and is replaced by the sound of a creek cascading down the mountain. The trail is well graded so it wasn't surprising to see a few trail runners in the early morning. The official trail ends at red line lake which is a scenic alpine lake with a peak looming over. This isn't the Pfeifferhorn itself but a false summit. The trail climbed up along loose dirt and scree behind the false peak where there were great views to the other side towards Salt Lake City and Mt Timpanogos to the south. After some pleasant walking is a short talus ridgeline before the Pfeifferhorn summit block which was probably the most difficult part of the entire hike. It involved scrambling large rocks but was quite fun. The steep hike up to the summit was somewhat short and the views from the top were great. On top were some energetic chipmunks jumping from rock to rock in search of food, and on the way down by the lake there was a marmot. Despite the packed trailhead parking lot the trail never felt overcrowded. Hikers were spaced out in the 5 miles and perhaps hiked on other connecting trails.

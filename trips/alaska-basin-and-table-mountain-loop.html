Trip Details

I was excited to hike into the Tetons from the opposite side near Idaho in the national forest. Overnight permits are not needed which saved headaches in trying to acquire them as they can be very competitive to get and limited in the national park. The hike began at the Teton Canyon trailhead which was pretty much full to the brim. The main lot was entirely full and I lucked out to get the second to last spot in the overflow lot. The trail started mostly flat for a few miles, passing through meadows with wildflowers and across streams. A few miles from the basin the grade steepened. At one point I stopped to snack for a few minutes, then after hiking for a minute ran into someone going the opposite way. The said a hundred feet ahead was a mama bear and two cubs drinking water from the stream. Hearing that I nearly ran forward to see if they were still there but did not see them after 0 minutes of waiting. Probably being a hot day they went back into the bushes. Eventually I made it to Alaska Basin up at 9600 ft and quickly set up camp as the sky grew grey and dark blue. There was some thunder and a brief spell of intense wind and rain which lasted only a few minutes. The sun came back out and quickly dried the tent.

After an early start the next day I hiked east up Alaska Basin to Buck Mountain pass and peered over to the other side. It looked out towards death canyon though there was a ledge that blocked much of the view so there wasn't too much to see. Up next was Table Mountain which first involved a 3.5 mile hike to Hurricane Pass, then an off trail ridge walk to the south slopes of Table Mountain, then a steep scramble over scree to the summit itself. The trail from Buck Mountain Pass to Sunset Lake was along mountain slopes but perched higher than the basin so there were nice views. All along the way were colorful wildflowers in all shades of pink, yellow, blue, red, and purple. Sunset lake was nice but there were many people camped out around it and several folks even swimming in it. After the lake the trail climbed towards Hurricane Pass, passing several creeks and carpets of wildflowers so dense you couldnt see the ground beneath them. It was incredible. Mile after mile of such color slowed down the hike considerably as I couldn't help but take photo after photo. Atop the pass several groups congregated, some had day packs, probably they were camped out by the lake and were doing a side trip. A few others had backpacks and were probably continuing down into the park. Another trail at the junction was an unmaintained use trail going along the ridge to Table Mountain which is what I took. The trail wasn't too difficult to follow and didnt actually go on the ridge but sidehilled just below it, over scree and at times pockets of snow. There wasn't anyone else hiking it. At the saddle to Table Mountain I found a steeper than expected route up. It was about a 900 ft climb in no more than a half mile on loose dirt and even looser rocks. From below I could see crowds of people on the summit who came up on the easier, official trail from the west. Finally I made it on top to many folks including nearly a dozen packed within a foot of each other at the highest point, which seems to occur often at popular peaks. The views were great though, north towards Cascade canyon, east toward the grand, middle, and south Tetons, south towards Alaska Basin, and west towards Idaho. I joined the main trail to head back to the parking lot. The last two miles were very steep with somewhat poor footing for such a popular trail. There was about a 2500 ft elevation loss in maybe 2 miles. The scenery today was spectacular and better than the previous day. But as good scenery as it was, what really made the hike memorable were the wildflowers. They were out of this world.

Trip Details

Less than five miles from the border with Mexico is the Miller Peak Wilderness. At lower elevations the wilderness area has lush green canyons with deciduous trees and flowing streams. At higher elevations are dense pine trees and oak among other trees, and a few creeks and springs. This hike started at the Ramsey trailhead which cost $8 to park, climbed up Wisconsin canyon to Bear Saddle at about 8,000 ft where it joined the Crest trail. From there I headed south over to Miller Peak, stopping at bathtub spring to fill up on water where it is piped from an underground spring out through the faucet, the continued up to the peak at 9,468 ft. Views on top were great, south into Mexico, east towards the town of Sierra Vista, then west, easily spotting Mt Wrightson on the horizon 40 miles away.

Just before dusk as clouds rolled in I headed up to Carr peak where, like Miller Peak, there were nice views of the wilderness. Clouds lingered overnight and a nice rainbow appeared at sunrise the next day. There was some very light drizzling rain for a few minutes which quickly moved away as I continued hiking. I headed back but at Bear Saddle continued on the Crest trail towards Ramsey Peak, encountering probably less frequented and overgrown trail. As the Crest trail (AZ trail) took an abrupt left west I continued on to towards Ramsey but opted out of hiking the 3 miles roundtrip there and back. The trail entirely disappeared and was replaced by shoulder high grass and debris which, towards the end of a two day hike, I couldn't summon the energy to navigate past. So I hiked to the top of Pat Scott peak then descended down the Pat Scott canyon, enjoying the many small waterfalls that ran the steep course down canyon, eventually reconnecting with the trail I took up. There was some cool wildlife along the way, including a Coati, large grasshopper, caterpillar, and many signs of bears though I ran into none.
